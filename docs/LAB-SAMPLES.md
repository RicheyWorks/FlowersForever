Notes:

* WSU's own soil guide says WSU does not run soil tests. It names UMass as accurate for western Washington soils,
  and points to WSU's list of labs. Soiltest Farm Consultants and A&L Western are included as regional
  commercial labs.
* The WSU Pullman plant clinic closed on December 1, 2025 and refers growers to Puyallup.
* **University of Idaho:** WSU's soil guide names it as accurate for western Washington soils. ASL's own page says
  it serves agricultural producers (and suggests commercial clients use a local independent lab). No
  out-of-state restriction is listed. It asks you to call or email before sending water samples.
* **Well water labs:** Water Management Laboratories and Spectra Laboratories – Kitsap appear on the Department of
  Health's list of certified nitrate and coliform labs (331-798, dated 3/27/2026) for Pierce and Kitsap counties.
  For other counties, use that list or Ecology's accredited lab search.

## Packing guides (in the app: Scouting & lab samples → How to send a sample)

* **Diseased plant** (WSU Puyallup):
  * Dig the plant up rather than pulling it, and include roots and soil.
  * Send both diseased and healthy tissue.
  * Don't send dead or rotted plants.
  * Double-bag the sample with no added water.
  * Fill in and sign the form.
  * Don't mail on a Friday or before a holiday.
  * Until it goes out, keep the sample in a refrigerator (WSU Whitman County Extension). Ask the lab before
    adding a cold pack.
* **Tape lifts and swabs:** none of these labs list tape lifts or swabs for plant disease, so the app tells growers to
  ask the lab first and send fresh tissue as well. WSU Puyallup says not to send insects stuck to tape or
  sticky traps.
* **Insects and mites:**
  * WSU Puyallup:
    * Freeze hard-bodied insects overnight.
    * Send them in a stoppered vial with tissue, inside a crush-proof box.
    * Alcohol cannot be mailed.
    * Never put butterflies, moths or bees in alcohol.
  * WSDA asks for 70–80% alcohol vials. Because alcohol can't be mailed, drop those off in person or ask the lab.
  * WSDA also asks for photos emailed to pest@agr.wa.gov first, with the insect kept in a clear container.
* **Soil** (OSU EC 628; WSU home soil guide; lab forms):
  * Take 15–20 subsamples per area to about 6 inches or tillage depth, using clean tools.
  * Don't use galvanized, brass or bronze tools if micronutrients are being tested.
  * Mix the subsamples and air-dry them, unless nitrate is being tested (then keep the sample cool and send it
    right away).
  * Use plastic zipper bags and label them.
  * Send about 2 cups for Soiltest or about 1 cup of dry soil for UMass.
* **Irrigation and well water** (Soiltest, A&L Western, University of Idaho, WA DOH, Ecology, Lewis County lab):
  * Decide the test first: irrigation suitability (pH, EC/salts, alkalinity, sodium/SAR, boron, chloride, nitrate)
    goes to an ag lab. Well water safety (coliform and nitrate, which DOH recommends testing yearly) goes to a
    Washington-accredited drinking water lab.
  * Use the lab's container: A&L wants a clean 16 oz plastic bottle for irrigation tests, Soiltest supplies
    containers, and bacteria tests need the lab's own treated bottle. Call the University of Idaho before
    sending water.
  * Check drop-off days and hold times first. Soiltest takes water Monday–Thursday only, and A&L wants it at the
    lab within 24–36 hours. Lewis County's lab wants coliform samples the same day they are collected.
  * Run the tap at least 5 minutes (Soiltest) or pump a well at least 10 minutes (A&L). Sample before filters and
    injectors, or before and after them to check the system. Don't sample a pond or ditch at the edge.
  * For chemistry, rinse the bottle three times with the water, then fill it to the top with no air. For bacteria,
    do not rinse.
  * Label each bottle by source name, keep it cool but not frozen, and pack the form and payment in the same box.
  * Test irrigation water yearly; A&L notes the end of summer often shows the worst quality.
