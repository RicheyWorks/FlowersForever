# Private-pilot known limitations

- Pull-request and manually dispatched candidate installers are deliberately unsigned, private, and not approved for customer distribution. Unsigned, low-reputation launchers can trigger SmartScreen, Gatekeeper, or antivirus heuristics. Do not override an antivirus threat block; stop and send the detection details to RicheyWorks for review. Tagged releases cannot publish until their signatures and notarization pass.
- Ordinary Actions artifact uploads remain quota-limited. An explicitly selected [private draft preview](PRIVATE_PREVIEW.md) can validate and retain unsigned installers without that artifact store; it never publishes the draft or changes signing requirements. Older August 28 preview manifests include an invalid self-checksum entry. Their six payload hashes were separately verified during backup; new manifests exclude themselves and must pass checksum verification.
- Norton's September 4 alert identified `FlowersForeverAI.exe` as `IDP.Generic`. The inspected local build has unsigned launchers; a false positive remains unconfirmed. Packaging now reuses tested app images and verifies matching publishers for signed Windows artifacts. The [Windows antivirus review](ANTIVIRUS_REVIEW.md) provides a metadata-only report and acceptance procedure; signing alone does not guarantee antivirus clearance.
- September 4 native validation passed readiness, stdio ping, and clean shutdown using the supported Windows JDK 17 app image and final verified JAR. Earlier attempts timed out while Windows suspended the JVM; the pausing mechanism was not identified or overridden. Startup deadlines can include host pauses. GitHub artifact quota separately blocked release-job input uploads; older archives were removed with owner approval, but quota refresh may take 6–12 hours. See [harness verification](HARNESS_AUDIT.md#farmer-entry-verification--september-4-2026).
- Tagged release publication is now technically gated on Microsoft Artifact Signing and Apple Developer ID notarization. The workflow is implemented, but external identity enrollment and protected GitHub environment values are still pending; see [native signing setup](SIGNING_SETUP.md).
- The supported customer database is local embedded H2, not a shared multi-workstation database. REST clients may share one running authenticated instance; they must not point separate app processes at copied database files.
- Farm setup records a desktop workspace, enterprise mix, and specialties, with Washington/Oregon as the first rollout and California labeled for later planning. Twenty-five farm-type choices change navigation while sharing one set of records. Current deep season planning, recipes, and production costs are built around cut flowers and stems. Cannabis, potato, apple, berry, vineyard, hay, hops, grains, seed, tree nut, vegetable, aquaculture, Christmas tree, mushroom, cranberry, pumpkin, nursery, and livestock tools hold local plans; poultry and dairy tools hold editable collections; Apiary farm holds dated Hive checks. A vineyard block does not record picked weight, grape quality, winery production, contract delivery, stock, or order links. A hay cutting plan does not count or weigh bales, calculate feed rations, grade forage, post stock, or link orders. A hop yard plan does not record yield, drying, pellets, stock, contracts, or orders. A grain field plan does not record actual yield, grade, moisture, storage, contracts, stock, or order links. A seed field plan does not calculate isolation or certify seed and does not record actual yield, cleaned lots, tests, stock, or order links. A nut block plan does not check pollinizer compatibility or record actual yield, cleaning, drying, grading, stock, or order links. A vegetable planting plan uses grower-entered dates without harvest prediction, and does not record actual yield, grading, wash/pack, storage, food-safety certification, stock, or order links. Existing Inventory and Customers & Orders remain generic. Generic harvest and stock records use whole-number quantities without weight or pack conversion. A flower shop has no point-of-sale register or purchasing workflow. Farm setup and specialty notebooks do not supply regulatory compliance or replace state tracking systems. See [farm types and workflow scope](FARM_TYPES.md).
- Aquatic cohorts are editable plans only. A planning stage does not verify species or site approval, a license, shellfish area-open status, water quality, or harvest authorization. The app does not issue shellfish tags, maintain regulatory traceability, record actual aquatic harvest, or post cohort plans to stock or orders. Requirements vary by species, site, and state; consult the relevant agencies. [Oregon commercial shellfish guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/food-safety/fs-licensing/Pages/commercial-shellfish.aspx) illustrates the separate licensing, area, and tag controls.
- Christmas tree blocks are editable plans only. They do not count actually cut trees, post inventory or customer orders, manage choose-and-cut bookings, or verify grower licensing, inspection, or export clearance. [Washington WSDA](https://agr.wa.gov/services/licenses-permits-and-certificates/plant-permits-and-certification-programs/christmas-trees) and [Oregon ODA](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/nursery-christmas-tree/Pages/nursery-licensing.aspx) publish separate state program information.
- Hive checks are beekeeper-entered observations, not colony diagnoses or treatment records. They do not verify registration or treatment, manage pollination contracts, record honey extraction, or create stock or sales links. [Washington WSDA](https://agr.wa.gov/services/licenses-permits-and-certificates/summary-descriptions/apiary-registration) and [Oregon ODA](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/ippm/Pages/bees-apiaries.aspx) publish separate registration information.
- Mushroom batches are editable plans by species, method, substrate, site, and grower-entered dates. A fruiting-window stage does not predict or confirm a flush. The notebook does not record actual harvested weight, control the growing environment, certify food safety, or post inventory or customer orders. [WSU Extension](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/product/a-hobbyist-guide-to-growing-shiitake-mushrooms-on-logs-for-woodland-owners/) and [OSU Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9364-nontimber-forest-products-small-woodland-owners-reishi-mushroom-growing-guide) describe distinct log and block methods that require their own operating decisions.
- Cranberry beds are editable perennial bed plans with grower-entered harvest method and dates. Wet and Dry are planning labels; the board does not control water or flooding, record actual harvested fruit, grade it, provide food-safety traceability, or post inventory or orders. [OSU Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9212-integrated-pest-management-strategic-plan-oregon-washington-cranberries) describes perennial beds and distinct wet and dry harvest practices.
- Pumpkin patches are editable season plans with grower-entered sales mode and visitor-area notes. A Pick your own or Farm stand mode does not issue tickets, take bookings, verify visitor arrangements, record actual harvest, or post inventory or sales. [OSU Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9909-adding-agritourism-developing-your-plan-working-your-county) lists pumpkin patches among agritourism activities, and its [U-pick guide](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9520-adding-u-pick-or-u-cut-options-your-farm) describes operational planning beyond a crop calendar.
- Desktop and AI processes on the same computer share H2 through its loopback-bound AUTO_SERVER listener. Bootstrap sets `h2.bindAddress=127.0.0.1` before H2 loads, avoiding the machine-hostname lookup that stalled macOS startup. An explicit JVM `-Dh2.bindAddress` setting is preserved; remote database sharing remains outside the supported customer configuration.
- The `/worker/` phone/tablet screen covers due production tasks, task actuals and optional photos, structured harvest lots (grade, marketable quantity, loss/culls, and storage), scoped physical inventory counts, and confirmed order handoffs due through the selected work date. Lot labels are printed or reprinted from the desktop/REST PDF route rather than a phone-native printer integration. Photos currently attach only to production tasks and accept JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, or HEIF up to 15 MiB. Counts and handoffs reject stale reviewed data instead of overwriting newer farm work; completing a handoff deducts its canonical inventory once, while an unchanged order already completed by another worker is reconciled without a second deduction. The pending queue belongs to one browser profile, exposes each queued entry for retry or discard, and retries when the page opens, the connection returns, or the worker chooses **Retry now**. Photo bytes use IndexedDB while operation metadata uses local storage; clearing site data before the pending count reaches zero discards those unsent device copies. Installable/offline shell caching requires HTTPS or localhost, and no retry runs after the browser is fully closed.
- Phone/tablet field notes save to the existing Knowledge Log and share the durable worker receipt mechanism. Notes support an observation, crop, bed, type, action, person, and follow-up; note photos are not implemented. A submitted draft remains tied to its original operation across reload and failed cleanup. Browser storage must be writable before dispatch, and clearing site data can still destroy unsent records. Recent notes show up to ten entries through the selected work date; use the desktop Knowledge Log for full search and editing.
- H2 upgrades use versioned Flyway migrations and Hibernate validation. Always take a pre-upgrade backup; downgrades still require restoring the matching pre-upgrade backup.
- SQLite remains a developer-preview compatibility profile and is not approved for customer data or covered by the H2 migration guarantee.
- Knowledge Log search is intentionally record-based: it matches ordinary words, tags, crops, beds, and month names, but does not infer diagnoses or generate advice. Seasonal comparisons, playbooks, reminders, and knowledge-report export are roadmap work.
- **Back up now** and **Automatic backups** run while the app stays open. **Restore from a backup** closes and reopens FlowersForever to swap the records in safely; it is not a fully in-place swap. Restore has been tested headlessly, not yet on a real Windows install. The PowerShell scripts still require the app to be closed. See [Backup and restore](BACKUP_RESTORE.md).
- Live connectors depend on third-party APIs and credentials. Failed connector work is reported and audited, but remote-side partial completion may require reconciliation in the provider's portal.
- Production Studio external handoffs are reviewed local records and provider-neutral payload briefs, not direct contract-signing, payment-processing, or marketplace-publishing clients. They store no payment credentials, access tokens, signatures, or legal contract bodies; complete and reconcile the action in the selected provider.
- The `farm-data` AI plugin exposes canonical records plus guided source-column suggestion, preview, validation, coercion, and atomic mapped import through a bundled MCP stdio adapter and the existing REST adapter. FlowersForever does not bundle provider login or file-reading clients: the farm's AI or integration service must still read rows from Excel, CSV, Google Sheets, or the previous app and checkpoint how far it has traversed that external source. Source-system and per-row source keys create durable database receipts inside FlowersForever. Dedicated domain tools now cover frost-aware planting preview/commit, task status and regeneration, recipe estimates, availability, and local provider handoffs. These production tools use ordinary process-local gateway replay; after a restart or evicted receipt, read current records before resubmitting. Availability and handoff pages are bounded responses over materialized service lists, not database-streamed exports.
- Native installers include and release-test a dedicated MCP stdio launcher plus a data/schema/tool readiness doctor, but each pilot farm's chosen AI client still needs a one-time local MCP registration and client-specific reconnect check. Portable JAR users must set `FLOWERFARM_DATA_DIR` explicitly when the AI client starts in a different working directory, or it will open a different `./data` store.
- The AI harness is an opt-in local integration boundary, not an autonomous farm operator. Its generic replay cache is bounded and process-local; source-keyed farm-data imports, complete farm-data exports, Optimus execution receipts, operation recovery, exclusive control leases, and staged-plan resume metadata have separate durable stores. Farm-data receipt replay is per source row rather than a permanent copy of the entire HTTP response. Complete exports are durable immutable snapshots under the configured data export directory, but they do not track later database changes; use a new stable key when a fresh snapshot is required. Durable Optimus execution replay requires an exact original request, including its deadline; other ordinary plugin responses remain process-local. The adapter must establish stable robot identity when the Optimus plugin starts. After that, durable execution replay, operation-history reads, and a degraded observation remain available during an owner-API outage, but new physical commands require live state. Offline nonterminal operation history is intentionally `UNKNOWN` rather than assumed to be running or complete. A replay, operation result, or event page with `reconciliationPersisted=false` remains readable but could not append its latest live or unconfirmed status; retry after storage recovers. Event pages never synthesize cursors for that unwritten state. Resuming a staged plan still requires `staged-plan-adapter-confirmed` to be true, including an exact adapter fingerprint match; if the robot adapter did not retain the same plan across its own reconnect or restart, the harness clears the stale record before execution and the client must stage again. Lease holder labels are authenticated only by the shared local harness credential and remain local, and no released owner transport is implemented. External IdP identity, per-user roles, provider credentials, durable non-robot job state, human approval, network egress, transcript retention, and physical safety validation still belong to the customer-selected adapter/deployment. Keep mutation and destructive policies off unless a supervised workflow explicitly needs them.
- Adapter timestamps still need to share a reasonably synchronized clock with the host. The lifecycle journal ignores samples older than its latest event and clamps decreasing progress, so a badly skewed owner API can delay reconciliation until its timestamps catch up.
- Irrigation falls back to Kitsap climatology when live weather is unavailable; it is operational guidance, not a soil-sensor or safety system.
- Native installers are built by release CI. Candidate CI also launches an app image on clean Windows and macOS runners before preserving either installer. Local MSI creation requires a compatible JDK/jpackage and WiX toolchain.
- The repository and release channel are private. Pilot installers may not be forwarded or redistributed.
- Automatic updates are implemented and tested headlessly, with the production publisher key and update address built in, but have not yet been exercised with real MSI upgrades. Each update shows one Windows permission prompt (per-machine MSI), and the macOS hand-off has not run on a Mac. MSI Authenticode signing is still pending. See [Automatic updates](AUTO_UPDATES.md).
