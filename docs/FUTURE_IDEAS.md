# What could make the next season easier?

Product ideas reviewed September 4, 2026. These are proposals, not shipped capabilities or release commitments. The order favors everyday grower value and builds on records FlowersForever already keeps.

## 1. A field notebook you can speak into

**Grower moment:** hands are busy, but the observation matters: “Bed three, zinnias, netting slipped after the wind.”

Start from the field-note capture and device queue now in the app. Add optional voice-to-draft entry that shows the transcript, crop, bed, and date before saving. Offer the ordinary text form when speech is unavailable. Never silently turn a transcript into a treatment instruction.

**Prove it:** a grower can correct a misheard crop, review a draft, and save exactly once after reconnecting. Measure entry completion time and corrections during a pilot.

## 2. Next week's stem promise

**First slice shipped** in File → [Stem demand board](STEM_DEMAND_BOARD.md): stems by crop and date from confirmed (optionally draft) orders, with live cooler stock, projected harvest, and shortages shown separately. Explicit crop/lot allocations and reservations remain later.

**Grower moment:** a florist wants forty bouquets next Friday. What is already promised, what is in the cooler, and what might be ready?

Join the existing availability outlook, order lines, and recipe requirements into a weekly demand board. Show three separate amounts: live uncommitted stock, existing commitments, and projected harvest. Add explicit crop/lot allocations before reserving supply; never treat a forecast as a confirmed stem.

**Prove it:** two buyers cannot reserve the same stock, a cancelled order releases its allocation, and a late crop exposes a shortage without rewriting the original promise.

## 3. Keep / trial / retire for each variety

**Grower moment:** seed ordering starts, and last summer's winner is a hazy memory.

Compare seasons using crop-and-bed harvest history, task labor, input costs, losses, and saved observations. Let the farmer mark a variety to keep, trial, or retire and record why. Keep unmatched harvests and missing labor visible. Production cost can be reported today; planting profit needs a reliable join to sales first.

**Prove it:** every number opens its supporting records, different growing windows are labeled, and missing evidence is never presented as a zero cost or failed crop.

## 4. The farm's own crew playbooks

**Grower moment:** a new helper needs to know how this farm cuts, conditions, and packs a particular flower.

Turn farmer-selected Knowledge Log entries into editable checklists with a named owner, revision date, and links to supporting lessons. Assign a reviewed checklist to production work. Keep general reference guidance separate from practices proven on this farm.

**Prove it:** a helper sees the current approved revision, completed work retains the revision used, and changing a checklist does not change historical work.

## 5. A bed map that helps make a decision

**Grower moment:** the next succession needs space, and the timeline shows a collision.

Add a simple farm-owned row/bed map with dimensions, paths, irrigation zones, and crop-family rotation history. Link each block to the existing Season Planner. Start with a practical diagram that can be printed; add aerial imagery only if pilots need it.

**Prove it:** moving a proposed planting explains capacity and timing conflicts before saving, and the same saved planting appears on both map and timeline.

## 6. One buying list for a busy event week

**First slice shipped** in the same [stem demand board](STEM_DEMAND_BOARD.md): order lines counted as saved recipes combine into one stem list across events, with stock shared once, plus copy and CSV export. Supplier and pack rounding, per-event grouping, substitutions, and printed workbench sheets remain later.

**Grower moment:** three events use overlapping recipes and the grower needs one sensible wholesale order.

Consolidate saved recipe requirements by item and supplier, apply pack rounding once to the combined need, and show existing stock, farm allocation, wholesale additions, and leftovers separately. Print workbench recipe sheets from the reviewed batch. Substitutions need grower approval and recalculated cost.

**Prove it:** combining two events never double-counts stock, removing an event recalculates the list, and the printed sheet matches the accepted recipe revision.

## 7. Cooler lots with useful shelf-life reminders

**Private-pilot slice shipped** in Help → Cooler lot review (aging board, crop intervals, count/quality/loss, QR labels). Lot-to-sale allocation and split/merge remain later.

**Grower moment:** which bucket should be packed first?

Build on existing harvest lot codes, grade, loss, storage, and QR labels. Let the farm set a review interval by crop and conditioning method. Show aging lots with their harvest date and storage location, then let the grower record a count, quality check, or loss. A reminder is not a guarantee of vase life.

**Prove it:** a lot can be traced through a reviewed split or merge, and a loss changes the correct inventory quantity once. Lot-to-sale allocation is a prerequisite for customer-level traceability.

## 8. Weather changes with an explainable plan

**Grower moment:** a cold night moves the planting window.

Combine saved frost assumptions, live weather when available, and crop templates into a proposed schedule change. Show the original dates, source timestamp, affected tasks, and proposed dates together. Leave recorded actuals intact; let the farmer accept individual changes.

**Prove it:** stale forecasts are labeled, the same proposal cannot apply twice, and completed work stays unchanged.

## How to choose the next increment

Pilot the field-entry improvements before adding more screens. Track whether growers can save a harvest, add a new product, find a saved note, recover an offline entry, and complete a handoff without assistance. Ask which repeated task costs them the most time each week. Prioritize an idea only when it shortens that task and the farm can inspect the result.

The [competitive review](COMPETITIVE_RESEARCH.md) identifies category expectations. The [roadmap](PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md) separates implemented slices from remaining work, and the [known limitations](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md) records deployment and integration boundaries.
