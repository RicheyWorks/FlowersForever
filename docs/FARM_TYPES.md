# Farm types and workflow scope

**The idea:** Choose the farm you run, or combine several types when your
operation spans more than one. The desktop brings those tools to the front while
customers, harvests, stock, orders, and field lessons stay together. Edit the
selection later as the operation changes.

FlowersForever is a private-preview app with a working cut-flower farm day:
plan beds, record stem harvests, manage stock, prepare floral recipes and customer
orders, review market packing, and keep field lessons. **Farm setup** records what
an operation grows and sells. The farm-type choice changes the starting view and
navigation. Cannabis, potato, apple, berry, cranberry, vineyard, hay and
forage, hops, grains, seed crops, tree nuts, vegetables, pumpkins, aquaculture,
Christmas trees, mushrooms, nursery, and livestock workspaces have limited local planning
notebooks.
Poultry & eggs and Dairy farm have editable local collection logs; Apiary farm
has a dated hive observation log. The table below describes what each choice
makes available today.

## Choose a type and describe the farm you actually run

At desktop startup, choose from twenty-five farm-type cards (twenty-four named types
plus Custom farm) or select **Combine farm types…**. A single choice is marked
**Current type**; a combination marks its
main card **Primary · in mix** and the other selected cards **In your mix**.
Use **Find a farm type** in the menu to narrow the visible cards. If no cards
match, **Clear search** shows them all again. Searching does not change the
saved selection.
Any role can enter the saved workspace or use **Open in mix** to jump to an
included type's tool without changing the selection. In a saved combination,
an OWNER can choose **Add to mix…** on an unselected type. The editor
includes that type in a draft with the existing mix; **Save combination** applies
the change. An OWNER can also edit the combination directly. Open
**File → Choose or combine farm types…** to return to the choices later. Use
**File → Farm setup…** to save a Washington or
Oregon location, one primary enterprise, any additional enterprises, specialties
such as crops or breeds, and flower offerings. A mixed farm can select the
specialty workspaces it actually uses and describe other business lines in setup
without being forced into one label.
California is listed as a later expansion area. These choices are saved with the
farm's local database and recovered by the [database backup](BACKUP_RESTORE.md).
Existing installations begin with Washington, cut flowers, and **Rose & events**
selected; review those defaults for Oregon or mixed farms. When the profile still
matches the original starter values, selecting a nonflower card replaces **Cut
flowers** and clears its flower offerings. A configured cut-flower or mixed farm
keeps its existing business lines and offerings. On that untouched starter,
**Custom farm** selects **Other** as the primary enterprise. Farm setup remains
the place to review the operation's details. A nursery selling living plants is
a different operation from a cut-flower farm, even when both grow the same species.

## Choose a workspace for the day's work

The workspace is a navigation choice for the whole operation, not a separate
database or a license to use an unfinished specialty workflow. Switching it
keeps the farm's customers, orders, inventory, harvest history, and notes
together. A farm that both grows flowers and runs a shop can record both
enterprises and combine the useful tools. The selected farm types are saved
separately from the broader enterprise mix recorded in Farm setup.
If a screen has unfinished work or a save is pending, finish that work before
switching from the farm-type menu. The menu checks this before saving a new
choice.
**Rose & events** keeps the familiar **Today** start; the other choices open a
**Workspace** overview linking to their available tools.

**Combine farm types…** lets an OWNER choose at least two farm types and
mark one as the main farm type. The combined overview opens first; the main type
sets context and shared defaults. The resulting workspace shows each selected
specialty tool and the shared farm records once. For example, selecting
**Potato farm**, **Cannabis farm**, and **Apple orchard** shows independent
**Field plans** and **Orchard blocks** notebooks alongside **Cultivation**;
general harvest, stock, orders, and notes stay shared. Reopen the combination
to add or remove types. Search in the combination dialog to narrow its farm
cards; **Clear search** restores all cards. Filtering leaves the selected mix
and main type intact. Drag cards onto the mix or main-focus targets, or use the
card checkboxes and **Main farm type** choice while searching. Adding
**Livestock farm** opens **Herd groups**; adding
**Poultry & eggs** opens **Egg collections**; adding **Dairy farm** opens
**Milk collections**; adding **Vineyard farm** opens **Vineyard blocks**; adding
**Hay & forage** opens **Hay cuttings**; **Hops farm** opens **Hop yards**;
**Grains farm** opens **Grain fields**; **Seed farm** opens **Seed fields**;
**Tree nut orchard** opens **Nut blocks**; **Vegetable farm** opens
**Vegetable plantings**; **Aquaculture farm** opens **Aquatic cohorts**;
**Christmas tree farm** opens **Tree blocks**; **Apiary farm** opens **Hive
checks**; **Mushroom farm** opens **Mushroom batches**; **Cranberry farm** opens
**Cranberry beds**; **Pumpkin patch** opens **Pumpkin patches**. Those records do not
turn into stock or sales transactions. **Custom farm** can join any mix for
unlisted work. It adds the shared Harvest Log, Inventory, and customer records,
with no separate production plan. Name the other crops in **Farm setup…** to
show them in the combined overview.

![Combined farm search showing Cannabis farm while the potato and cannabis mix stays selected](images/app-combined-farm-search.png)

On the farm menu, **Open in mix** goes straight to a selected type's tool and
keeps the combination saved. **Add to mix…** on an unselected card opens
the editor with the current types and that card in a draft; **Save combination**
applies it. This includes **Custom farm**; it can also be the main farm type
while the named specialty tools remain available.
Removing a type hides its tool but keeps its saved plans so adding it later can
bring the plans back. Finish unfinished drafts and pending saves before applying
a different combination. An existing combination can be edited down to one
type when the operation no longer needs the other tools.

Combining farm types collects the tools in these rows without changing their
scope. A cannabis planning batch remains separate from the generic Harvest Log
and Inventory even when other crop types are selected. Field and orchard plans
remain separate records, with their own editable names, dates, acreage, stages,
and notes. Products, crop names, and other supported plan details can be edited;
the combination does not create arbitrary custom fields or automatic links
between plans, harvests, stock, and sales. Herd groups are likewise independent
of inventory and customer orders; edits replace the current planning details
rather than recording a movement or animal history. Egg collections are dated
entries that can be edited, including multiple entries for a flock on one day;
their usable count is a calculation, not stock posted to Inventory. Milk
collections are separate dated entries: recording or editing a milking session
does not alter a herd group, create dairy stock, or post an order. The unit
recorded for each session stays explicit; the app does not convert lb, gal, and L.
Vineyard blocks are seasonal plans rather than actual harvest or winery records;
they stay separate from the generic Harvest Log, Inventory, and orders.
Hay cuttings are likewise separate plans. Adding Hay & forage to a dairy or
livestock combination makes the cutting notebook available without changing
milk entries, herd groups, feed stock, or orders. Adding Hops farm to a mix
shows Hop yards alongside the other selected tools; its seasonal plans do not
post harvested hops, stock, contracts, or orders.
Adding Grains farm shows Grain fields beside the other selected tools. A saved
field can start a next-rotation draft: its field and acres carry forward, its
grain crop becomes the previous crop, and the next crop, variety, dates, and
notes are cleared. The grower chooses a new crop and planned harvest start
before saving. Rotation plans do not post harvested grain, stock, contracts,
or orders.
Adding Seed farm shows Seed fields in the same mix. A saved seed field can start
a next-season draft with its field, crop, variety, and acres carried forward and
its dates cleared. The grower enters a new planned harvest start before saving.
Isolation notes are a place to write the grower's own observations; the app does
not calculate distances, check neighboring fields, certify seed, post cleaned
lots, or connect a plan to stock or orders.
Adding Tree nut orchard shows Nut blocks beside the other selected tools. A
saved block can start a next-season draft with its block, crop, variety,
pollinizer variety, acres, tree count, and destination carried forward. Planned
dates and notes are cleared; the grower enters a new planned harvest start
before saving. Pollinizer details are grower-entered. The app does not check
compatibility or post a nut harvest, stock, or order from this plan.
Adding Vegetable farm shows Vegetable plantings in the mix. A saved planting
can start a next-succession draft with its field or bed, crop, variety, acres,
intended channel, and previous-crop note carried forward, its succession
number increased, and its dates and notes cleared. The grower sets the new
timing before saving. A planting plan does not forecast harvest readiness or
post produce to stock or customer orders.
Adding Aquaculture farm shows Aquatic cohorts beside the other selected tools.
A saved cohort can start a **Plan next cohort** draft: species, growing site,
culture method, and intended destination carry forward; cohort name, dates,
and notes clear; and stage returns to Planned. The grower supplies a new name
and planned harvest start. A stage move does not indicate that a site is
approved or open, authorize harvest, or post stock or customer orders.
Adding Christmas tree farm shows Tree blocks beside the other selected tools.
**Plan replacement planting** carries the saved block name, tree species,
acres, target height, and intended channel into a new draft. It clears
planting and harvest years, planted count, dates, and notes, and resets the
stage to Planned. The grower supplies a new target harvest year before
saving. A planning stage does not count cut trees or post inventory or sales.
Adding Apiary farm shows Hive checks beside orchard, berry, and other selected
tools. **Start next check** carries the saved apiary/site, hive label, and
purpose into a new draft, clearing the check and next-check dates, status,
observations, and actions. The beekeeper supplies a new check date before
saving. A status move updates only that saved check; it does not diagnose or
treat a colony or post honey to inventory or orders.
Adding Mushroom farm shows Mushroom batches beside the other selected tools.
**Plan next batch** carries the species, growing method, substrate, growing
area, and intended channel into a new draft. Batch name, dates, and notes
clear, and stage returns to Planned. The grower supplies a new batch name
before saving. A planning stage does not record a fruiting event, harvested
weight, or finished mushroom stock.
Adding Cranberry farm shows Cranberry beds beside the other selected tools.
**Plan next season** carries the bed name, variety, acres, harvest method,
and intended channel into a new draft. Planned dates and notes clear, and
stage returns to Planned. A saved bed does not post an actual wet or dry
harvest, stock, or order.
Adding Pumpkin patch shows Pumpkin patches beside other selected tools.
**Plan next season** carries the patch name, variety, acres, planned sales
mode, and visitor-area notes into a new draft. Planting and picking dates
and general notes clear, and stage returns to Planned. A saved plan does not
book visitors, record picked pumpkins, or post stock and sales.

Flower offerings (stems, bouquets, weddings) describe what the operation sells.
**Floral retail** lets a flower shop identify itself without claiming it grew
the flowers; a wildflower grower remains a farm rather than a generic shop. The
categories give each a useful entrance without splitting a mixed operation's
records.

The growing vocabulary follows the [USDA Census of Agriculture's crop,
horticulture, and livestock tables](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/AgCensus/2022/Full_Report/Volume_1%2C_Chapter_1_State_Level/Oregon/).
Floral retail is a separate shop choice, not a crop category.
Washington's [agricultural profile](https://cms.agr.wa.gov/WSDAKentico/Documents/Pubs/AGR5-2503-002-WSDAAgInfographic-WEB.pdf)
highlights apples, cattle, milk, potatoes, hay, wheat, eggs, hops, onions, and
grapes. Oregon's [agricultural facts](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/Documents/Publications/Administration/ORAgFactsFigures.pdf)
show greenhouse and nursery crops, cattle, hay, grass seed, milk, wine grapes,
potatoes, wheat, onions, eggs, berries, hazelnuts, and pears. These are examples
for farm classification and future research, not a list of supported workflows.
Washington identifies blueberries and raspberries among its leading crops in its
[2026 agriculture proclamation](https://cms.agr.wa.gov/WSDAKentico/Documents/DO/Communications/WashingtonAgricultureMonth-Proclamation-2026.pdf).
Oregon's [berry overview](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/agriculture/Pages/about-oregon-agriculture.aspx)
names blueberries, blackberries, marionberries, raspberries, strawberries, and
other berries. One Berry farm choice can hold these different crops in named blocks.
Seed production has its own field-planning needs: [WSU Extension describes
vegetable seed-crop isolation work in Washington's Columbia Basin](https://extension.wsu.edu/grant/agriculture/potato-vegetable-production-systems/cbvsa-cbvsfra/),
and [OSU Extension covers grass, legume, and other field seed crops in Oregon](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/marion/field-crops).
The Seed farm notebook records plans and manual isolation notes; it does not
apply either program's standards or determine whether a field qualifies.
The Washington and Oregon picker gives tree nut orchards their own workspace.
[Oregon's agriculture overview names hazelnuts among its leading commodities](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/agriculture/pages/about-oregon-agriculture.aspx),
and [OSU Extension describes hazelnut orchard design and pollinizer placement](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9077-growing-hazelnuts-pacific-northwest-orchard-design).
The Nut blocks notebook records a grower's plan; it does not apply OSU's
orchard recommendations or evaluate pollinizer compatibility.
Vegetable growers need room to schedule repeat plantings. [WSDA's agriculture
profile lists onions among Washington's major crops](https://cms.agr.wa.gov/WSDAKentico/Documents/Pubs/AGR5-2503-002-WSDAAgInfographic-WEB.pdf),
and [Oregon's agriculture overview includes onions and processed vegetables](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/agriculture/pages/about-oregon-agriculture.aspx).
[OSU Extension describes the value of adjusting planting and harvest schedules
to weather](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/impact/croptime-helps-vegetable-growers-fine-tune-planting-harvest-schedules).
Vegetable plantings stores grower-entered dates; it does not run OSU's
Croptime models or predict crop development.
Aquaculture needs its own planning vocabulary. [WSDA identifies shellfish
aquaculture as a Washington agricultural sector](https://agr.wa.gov/departments/agricultural-products/aquaculture),
while [Oregon's aquaculture guide covers fish, shellfish, other aquatic animals,
and aquatic plants](https://www.oregon.gov/ODA/Documents/Publications/MarketAccess/AquacultureUsersGuide.pdf).
The cohort notebook deliberately stores the grower's plan, not a finding that
a species, culture method, or site is approved. [Washington DOH lists separate
commercial shellfish licenses and operation requirements](https://doh.wa.gov/community-and-environment/shellfish/commercial-shellfish/licensing-information),
and [Oregon's commercial shellfish
guidance describes area classification, licensing, water quality, and tagging](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/food-safety/fs-licensing/Pages/commercial-shellfish.aspx);
those checks and records remain outside this preview.
Christmas tree production has its own block and season work. [WSDA describes
Washington Christmas tree growers, including choose-and-cut and wholesale
farms](https://agr.wa.gov/services/licenses-permits-and-certificates/plant-permits-and-certification-programs/christmas-trees),
and [ODA distinguishes harvested Christmas trees from living nursery stock](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/nursery-christmas-tree/Pages/nursery-licensing.aspx).
[OSU Extension describes shearing and harvest planning in Pacific Northwest
Christmas tree production](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pnw-684-developing-quality-christmas-trees-pacific-northwest).
Tree blocks hold grower-entered targets; they do not apply the extension
recommendations or check license, inspection, and shipping requirements.
[Washington's apiary registration program](https://agr.wa.gov/services/licenses-permits-and-certificates/summary-descriptions/apiary-registration)
and [Oregon's bees and apiaries program](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/ippm/Pages/bees-apiaries.aspx)
describe separate state registration requirements. [OSU Extension identifies
pollination services and honey production as major Pacific Northwest
beekeeping activities](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/pnw-742-revenue-sources-commercial-beekeeping-operation-pacific-northwest).
Hive checks hold the beekeeper's dated observations; they do not verify
registration, assess contracts, diagnose colonies, prescribe treatment, or
connect honey extraction to stock or sales.
[WSU Extension describes forest-cultivated shiitake on logs in the Pacific
Northwest and distinguishes it from indoor sawdust-bag growing](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/product/a-hobbyist-guide-to-growing-shiitake-mushrooms-on-logs-for-woodland-owners/).
[OSU Extension describes both inoculated logs and fruiting blocks for
reishi](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9364-nontimber-forest-products-small-woodland-owners-reishi-mushroom-growing-guide).
Mushroom batches let the grower name a method and substrate without assuming
one production system for all species. Their dates are plans, not a forecast
from either extension guide, and their stages do not post harvests or stock.
[OSU Extension describes perennial cranberry beds and the distinction between
wet harvest for processing and dry harvest for fresh fruit](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9212-integrated-pest-management-strategic-plan-oregon-washington-cranberries).
[WSU Extension documents cranberry production in Washington](https://extension.wsu.edu/graysharbor/ag/cranberry/).
Cranberry beds use grower-entered method and date plans; they do not control
water, record harvested fruit, or post stock or orders.
[OSU Extension lists pumpkin patches among farm agritourism activities](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9909-adding-agritourism-developing-your-plan-working-your-county)
and [describes planning for U-pick sales and visitors](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9520-adding-u-pick-or-u-cut-options-your-farm).
Pumpkin patches can name pick-your-own, farm stand, wholesale, or mixed plans,
while visitor-area notes remain grower-entered notes rather than bookings or
visitor checks.
Oregon's [nursery licensing guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/oda/nursery-christmas-tree/Pages/nursery-licensing.aspx)
and Washington's [flower-selling guidance](https://agr.wa.gov/departments/business-and-marketing-support/small-farm/the-green-book/regulations-for-specific-products)
distinguish living nursery stock, such as rooted starts and plants for
propagation, from cut flowers. Nursery farm plans living plants; Flower shop and
the cut-flower workspaces handle their existing products separately.

The [USDA Census of Horticultural Specialties](https://data.nass.usda.gov/Publications/AgCensus/2022/Online_Resources/Census_of_Horticulture_Specialties/index.php)
also separates cut flowers, cut cultivated greens, nursery stock, bulbs, and
protected food crops. That is why “flowers” should allow more detail than one
undifferentiated crop label.

## What the choices mean today

Custom categories and units help a farm name its own products. The current
inventory ledger keeps whole-number quantities and does not convert pounds,
bags, bins, or other packs. The existing Season Planner and floral recipe
calculations still assume a stem-based workflow; the potato, apple, berry,
vineyard, hay, hops, grains, seed, nut, vegetable, nursery, aquaculture,
Christmas tree, mushroom, cranberry, pumpkin, and livestock notebooks are separate local plans; the poultry,
dairy, and apiary notebooks hold dated observations or collections. A potato,
apple, berry, vineyard, hay, hops, grains, seed, nut, vegetable, nursery,
aquaculture, Christmas tree, mushroom, cranberry, pumpkin, apiary, livestock, poultry, or dairy choice
must not be read as support for that enterprise's full production, food-safety,
or sales process.

For roses, the practical current offerings are cut stems, bunches, and floral
designs made from named products. The rose variety guide and visualizer are
reference and design tools. The [USDA horticulture survey instructions](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Surveys/Guide_to_NASS_Surveys/Census_of_Horticultural_Specialties/2024_Census_of_Horticultural_Specialties_Instruction_Sheet.pdf)
recognize stems, bunches, and spikes as different cut-flower reporting units;
the farm should keep its own selling unit explicit.
Bouquet recipes estimate stem needs and economics; they do not reserve or consume
stems or create bouquet stock. Record assembled bouquets as separate inventory
products and counts before fulfilling bouquet orders.

## Cannabis and hemp require a separate track

A first cannabis workspace records **cultivation plans** by batch, area, stage,
and plant count. It gives the operator a separate starting point and access to
the existing generic inventory and customer-order tools. The batch notebook
lets an operator drag a saved batch onto a planning stage, or select the batch
and click the stage. This saves the stage change immediately after an open draft
has been saved or discarded. The stage is only a local planning label. It
does not post harvests into stock, reserve products for orders, or reconcile
sales back to plants. Detailed cultivation event history, harvest and finished
product inventory, sales reconciliation, and regulatory traceability remain separate
design and verification work before FlowersForever could be used as a regulated
system of record. Testing, transfers, manifests, and state reporting are not
implemented.
The app's ordinary harvest lot code or QR label is not cannabis compliance
traceability.

Washington's [Cannabis Central Reporting System](https://lcb.wa.gov/ccrs)
has separate [plant, harvest, inventory, transfer, product, and sale reporting
schemas](https://lcb.wa.gov/ccrs/resources). Oregon requires OLCC licensees to
use its [Cannabis Tracking System, Metrc](https://www.oregon.gov/olcc/marijuana/pages/olcccts.aspx),
for seed-to-sale tracking. Industrial hemp follows a distinct licensing path in
[Washington](https://agr.wa.gov/departments/agricultural-products/hemp), while
[Oregon distinguishes ODA hemp from OLCC cannabis](https://www.oregon.gov/olcc/marijuana/Pages/Adult-Use-Hemp-FAQ.aspx)
and has separate rules when hemp enters the OLCC channel. A farm profile may
name either enterprise; it does not satisfy any of these requirements.

## Staged expansion

1. **Washington and Oregon first:** validate Farm setup, combined navigation,
   and the existing cut-flower workflow with small farms, including mixed
   operations. Use the selected specialty mix and broader enterprise list to
   choose specific next workflows with growers.
2. **Selected specializations next:** validate the potato field, apple orchard,
   berry block, vineyard block, hay cutting, hop yard, grain field, seed field, nut block, vegetable planting, pumpkin patch, aquatic cohort, Christmas tree block, mushroom batch, cranberry bed, hive check, nursery batch, livestock group, egg collection, and milk collection notebooks
   with growers, then
   research and test the production, packout, storage, and animal-record tasks
   those operators actually need before claiming those workflows.
   Extend the cannabis planning notebook only after
   validating cultivation and sales tasks with licensed operators, and keep a
   separate jurisdiction-specific release gate for regulated features.
3. **California later:** review local farm needs and requirements before adding
   it as a supported setup location. [California Department of Food and
   Agriculture statistics](https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/Statistics/) describe a
   larger, different commodity mix; the WA/OR templates should not be assumed
   to fit every California farm.
